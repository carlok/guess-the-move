- Installable and offline (PWA, owner backlog item 9): `web/manifest.webmanifest`, an app icon (the cburnett knight on a board pattern, `tools/make_icons.js`, credited in `NOTICE`), and a service worker (`web/sw.js`) that a build (`make dist`) stamps with its build id and the exact URLs of that build. Rules, each tested in a fake worker scope (`web/test/sw.test.js`): one cache per build, deleted when the next build activates; scripts and styles are cached by their exact versioned URL, so one build's `app.js` can never be paired with another's page; the page and the dataset go network-first with the cache as the offline fallback; the optional api and anything that is not a same-origin GET are never touched; a failed response is never cached; a new build waits until the page asks it to take over (a "Reload" bar, `web/pwa.js`), so a page is never switched under the user. In the source tree (placeholders left) the worker does nothing, so development never meets a stale cache. It registers only on https or localhost. Real-browser test against `dist/` (`e2e/pwa.spec.js`, part of `make dist-check`): the worker caches the build, then the page reloads with the network off and a move can be played. **The web container image now copies `dist/`** (`make web-image` builds it first), replacing the hand-kept file list whose omissions caused the earlier blank-board bug.

Ending early gives a shorter report. The last session is kept for the stats page.
- Retry your mistakes (spaced repetition): a best-move guess that gives up 10 or more win-% points queues the position; it comes back after 5, then 15, then 40 more positions (Leitner boxes counted in plays, `web/srs.js`), leaves the queue after four good retries in a row (a retry that is a mistake again starts over, an inaccuracy waits the same time again). At most one position in three is a retry, retries respect the phase and kind filters but not "unseen only", and wear a Retry chip. Predict mode neither queues nor retries. Setting "Bring back positions I got wrong" (on by default). The stats page shows how many are due and "Retry one now". Retries never count for the level estimate (first look only). Saved in the stats file (`srs`, `plays`, validated, merged on load) and cleared by Reset stats. Queue entries whose position is not in the loaded dataset are ignored and not counted.
- Deep links: `#p=<position id>` opens that position, `#p=<id>&m=<move>` opens a shared answer as a review of that move (labelled "Shared answer", never written to your history); a link icon next to the timer copies the position link, "Copy a link to my answer" on the reveal copies the answer link; an unknown id says so and shows another position, a move that is not legal there is ignored, a malformed link is ignored; the address is cleaned after use so a reload does not repeat it. Without a clipboard (an insecure page) the link is shown to copy by hand.
- Move tiers (owner request: a chess.com-style scale from Brilliant to Blunder on all legal moves): `web/classify.js` assigns every legal move a tier from its cost, so the scale is relative to the position: Best (< 0.1 win-% points below the best move), Excellent (<= 2), Good (< 5), Inaccuracy (< 10), Mistake (< 20), Blunder (>= 20); Great = the best move when the runner-up costs >= 15 (the dataset's `only_move` rule); Brilliant = a best-or-nearly-best move that is a real sacrifice (an exchange on the destination square that costs a minor piece net of the capture), in a position not already won (best win% <= 90) and not lost after (eval >= 0). `web/tierview.js` draws the badge, a distribution bar (one slot per legal move, grouped by tier, yours marked) and the list of all legal moves (a button each: tap to draw the move on the board). The reveal shows your tier and how many of the other moves yours beats, every table row carries its tier, the tier is saved with each guess (older guesses get theirs from the dataset), the stats page has "Your moves by tier", and finding a Great or Brilliant move in best-move mode plays a short fanfare. Own icons: colour discs with a glyph and always the name (annotation symbols as text, Lucide star / thumbs-up / check); chess.com's artwork is not used. Verified: the attack detector equals python-chess on every square of 68 positions (`tests/attack_vectors.json`, regenerated by `tests/gen_attack_vectors.py`), the thresholds equal `pipeline/tags.py` (`tests/test_classify_thresholds.py`). On the 2,000 shipped positions (`docs/research/tier-report.json`, `tools/tier_report.js`): 63,439 legal moves are 0.04% Brilliant, 0.11% Great, 3.5% Best, 5.5% Excellent, 10.8% Good, 16.1% Inaccuracy, 20.2% Mistake, 43.8% Blunder; 27 positions (1.35%) have a Brilliant move, 67 a Great one (all 67 carry the `only_move` tag, of 72 tagged). Approximations, stated: chess.com's own bands depend on the player's rating (not reproduced), no Book or Miss tier, pins and x-rays are ignored in the exchange, so some tactical captures count as sacrifices that a full engine would not.

- Level estimate in the browser (owner request: a mathematical way to infer the rating, weighting positions): `positions.json` now carries Maia-3's move probabilities at five rating anchors per position (`maia.by_elo`, 1000/1300/1600/1900/2200, the real opponent's rating, moves under 0.3% dropped; +0.5 MB gzipped). `web/level.js` interpolates log-linearly between anchors, multiplies the likelihood of your moves over a rating grid (1000-2200, step 50) and tempers it (power 1/1.5). Positions are weighted by what they can tell: mutual information in bits between the move and the rating; a move all ratings agree on counts for nothing. The stats page shows "Your level" (estimate, 80% range, density, estimate as you play); only first-time best-move guesses count (retries and positions first met in predict mode do not); "Suggest a position that sharpens my estimate" and an optional setting pick the position with the most information. `tools/add_anchors.py` adds anchors to an existing dataset by replaying each game from the archive for its history (all 2,000 positions reproduced the stored Maia probabilities to 5e-5, i.e. rounding), `--maia-anchors` does it at build time, `tools/anchor_probe.py` + `tools/level_eval.js` measure it. URL streaming now resumes from the byte reached after a stall (the first archive read timed out).
- How good is it (docs/research/level-eval.json, real players of known rating, real moves as guesses): interpolation is fine (mean error 0.003 in probability). The estimate is rough: pooled over players rated 1300-1800 the 80% range held the truth 88/85/80/76% of the time after 5/10/20/40 guesses and was 765/675/561/427 points wide; mean error 148/141/136/123 against 152/151/151/151 for always guessing 1600. Untempered coverage was only 80/75/71/69%. Estimates ran about 90 below the players' Lichess Rapid ratings, and players rated 1900 came out near 1620-1650 (no better than a constant guess). Three likelihoods were tried (exact move probability, Gaussian on move quality, kernel-smoothed on move quality): same bias, so it is the model-vs-people gap, not the likelihood form. Choosing positions by information, with a synthetic player that behaves exactly as the model says, cut the error and the range by about a third after 20 guesses; that says nothing about real people. The page says all of this.
