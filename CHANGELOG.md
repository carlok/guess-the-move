- Level estimate in the browser (owner request: a mathematical way to infer the rating, weighting positions): `positions.json` now carries Maia-3's move probabilities at five rating anchors per position (`maia.by_elo`, 1000/1300/1600/1900/2200, the real opponent's rating, moves under 0.3% dropped; +0.5 MB gzipped). `web/level.js` interpolates log-linearly between anchors, multiplies the likelihood of your moves over a rating grid (1000-2200, step 50) and tempers it (power 1/1.5). - How good is it (docs/research/level-eval.json, real players of known rating, real moves as guesses): interpolation is fine (mean error 0.003 in probability). The estimate is rough: pooled over players rated 1300-1800 the 80% range held the truth 88/85/80/76% of the time after 5/10/20/40 guesses and was 765/675/561/427 points wide; mean error 148/141/136/123 against 152/151/151/151 for always guessing 1600. Untempered coverage was only 80/75/71/69%. Estimates ran about 90 below the players' Lichess Rapid ratings, and players rated 1900 came out near 1620-1650 (no better than a constant guess). Three likelihoods were tried (exact move probability, Gaussian on move quality, kernel-smoothed on move quality): same bias, so it is the model-vs-people gap, not the likelihood form. Choosing positions by information, with a synthetic player that behaves exactly as the model says, cut the error and the range by about a third after 20 guesses; that says nothing about real people.
