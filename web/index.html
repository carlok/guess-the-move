Positions where you gave up 10 or more win-% points come back after 5, then 15, then 40 positions, until you get them right four times. At most one position in three is a retry.

Sharpening chooses positions where players of different ratings disagree most, so fewer guesses are needed. Matching chooses positions that about half of the players at your estimated level solve; "a bit harder" ones that fewer do. Both need five counted guesses before they have a level to go by (until then positions are random) and apply in "Find the best move" mode.

Useful on touch screens, where a slip of the finger would reveal the answer.

How it works

Your statsSettingsHow it worksAbout

You see a real position from a real game between players of a known rating, and you guess a move. Then the app shows four moves side by side:

  • Yours.
  • Stockfish's. The objectively best move, as judged by the strongest open-source engine.
  • The real player's. What a person of that rating actually played.
  • Maia's. What a typical player of that rating would play, according to a model trained on millions of human games.

The interesting part is where they disagree. If the real player and Maia differ, the player did something unusual. If Maia and Stockfish differ, that is a typical human mistake at that level. If the real player found Stockfish's move but Maia would not, it was a hard one to find.

What the numbers mean

Cost
How much chance of winning a move gives up compared with the best move, in percentage points (using the Lichess win-probability curve). Lower is better.
Move quality
That cost turned into a score from 0 to 100 with the Lichess accuracy formula. Shown in "Find the best move" mode.
Closeness
How near your move is to another move, measured the same way; 100 means the same move. Shown in "Predict the real move" mode.
Plausible
The share of players of the real player's rating who would choose that move, according to Maia.

Good to know

  • Previous (or p) reopens an earlier position of this visit to study it again: the timer stops at the time you took, the full answer is shown, and nothing is recorded a second time. Try again plays it afresh with the timer reset; Next walks forward through the ones you reviewed and then suggests a new position.
  • Your level, on the stats page, is estimated from your first-time guesses in "Find the best move" mode: your moves are compared with how often players of each rating choose them (Maia-3), and positions where ratings disagree count for more. It is a Lichess-equivalent rating with an 80% range, not a FIDE rating, and it assumes you choose like a player of your rating does in a game.
  • A timer runs on each position and starts again with the next one; it pauses when you leave the tab or open a view, and its time goes into your stats. You can hide it in the settings.
  • Moves and results have sounds, made in your browser. The speaker button in the top bar mutes them.
  • Nothing is calculated while you play. Every legal move of every position was scored in advance, so the page works with no server.
  • Your statistics stay in this browser. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Move by tapping a piece and then its square, by dragging it, or with the keyboard (arrow keys, Enter, Escape). On touch screens you confirm before the answer is revealed; you can change that in the settings.
